# Virtara: 5 clients in 30 days, on almost no budget

Written 11 November 2026. The window runs to 11 December, which matters: most South African businesses wind down from about 15 December, so decisions have to be made by roughly 5 December.

**Honest odds.** Five new paying clients in 30 days from a standing start is a stretch. With the plan below, two to four is realistic and five is possible if the warm base produces a referral early. I count a client as anyone who pays a deposit or a first monthly fee.

## 1. Where you stand

What I read: the Virtara site and repo, the AgentOS traction data (`~/.agentos-ui/traction/state.json`), the four law firm emails and the preview sites they link to.

**Strengths you are under-using**
- **Preview sites that are genuinely good.** I looked at the Burrows and Tanners drafts. They are better than most agency portfolios, and you can produce one fast. This is your unfair advantage.
- **A law firm client already.** Kochukov & Blume (kblegal.co.za) is live, has a case study and runs Concierge. None of the four law firm emails mention it.
- **Six live client sites** (Vaja, PL Steel, MPower, Aureya, KB Legal, Bonded) and no public reviews from any of them.
- **A fast, low-risk offer:** live in 3–6 working days, 50% deposit, and the client sees the homepage before paying anything.
- **A lead engine** in AgentOS that finds businesses and audits their sites.

**Weaknesses that cost you sales**
- **Volume is tiny.** The CRM shows 20 prospects, 8 contacted, 0 replies, and a target of 20 outreach a week that is not being met. The "Website Build" experiment aimed for 10 contacts in 11 days.
- **One touch, one channel.** Every prospect got one email. Nobody was phoned.
- **Two price lists.** The site sells builds at R12,500 once-off. The outreach emails offer R3,129 setup plus R1,470–R2,570 a month. A prospect who clicks through to virtara.co.za sees a different business.
- **No reviews, no Google Business Profile.**
- **The ICP is "small businesses in Johannesburg"**, which is too wide to write a sharp message for. The sweep even pulled in Hogan Lovells and Pinsent Masons.

## 2. The four law firm emails

**First: it is too early to call them failures.** They went out between 5 and 9 November, so they are two to six days old. Cold email typically gets a reply from a few in every hundred. Four emails with no follow-up should be expected to produce no replies. The lesson is about volume and follow-up before it is about wording.

That said, each email has problems that would hold the reply rate down at any volume.

| Firm | Sent to | What went wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Harris Marcus Mahlangu | chareen@ (not clearly a partner) | Opens with "Your firm's sophisticated legal practice deserves…", which reads as machine-written flattery. The complaint is a © 2015 footer, which costs a Sandton property firm nothing. The link is a raw deployment address with a random string in it. |
| Snaid & Morris | info@ | The best-targeted of the four (family and labour clients, no enquiry form), but it went to a general inbox, the subject says "Morris Law", which is not the firm's name, and it quotes R3 129 and R2 570 before they have seen anything. |
| Tanners & Associates | admin@, on a Friday at 18:00 | Real, specific faults (broken navigation, template labels showing), but the email opens by listing them, which reads as criticism. Sent at the worst time of the week to a shared inbox. |
| Burrows Attorneys | david@ | Subject line "Visitors may have to search for your contact form" is a complaint, not a reason to open. Burrows is a corporate and commercial firm that wins work by referral, so a contact form is not their problem. |

**The problems they share, most damaging first**

1. **No proof.** You have a law firm client with a live site and a case study, and no email mentions it. To an attorney, "we did this for Kochukov & Blume, here is their site" is worth more than any audit finding.
2. **The best asset is hidden.** The preview is the whole pitch, but it sits behind a link. Attorneys are trained not to click links from strangers, and a `…-4jjqkipgl-virtec-projects.vercel.app` address looks exactly like the phishing they are warned about.
3. **It looks like it came from a personal Gmail.** AgentOS is connected to send from 2610dylan@gmail.com, while a virtara.co.za mailbox exists. Check your Sent folder; if these went from Gmail, that alone would sink them with a law firm.
4. **Wrong person.** `info@` and `admin@` reach a receptionist. The buyer is a partner or the practice manager.
5. **The problem is not one they feel.** Footer dates and missing meta descriptions are things a web developer notices. A partner cares about enquiries, about looking as good as the firm down the road, and about not being embarrassed when a client looks them up.
6. **Price too early, and odd.** R3,129 and R1,670 look generated, differ from firm to firm, and turn a first hello into a quote. A low price can also read as low quality to a firm that bills by the hour.
7. **Presumption.** The Burrows draft promises that "an attorney will phone you, free" on the firm's behalf and uses their logo. Lawyers are sensitive to both, and to Legal Practice Council advertising rules. Say plainly that it is a private concept using their public content.
8. **No follow-up and no phone call.**
9. **Half the list is the wrong kind of firm.** Commercial firms in Sandton live on referrals. Consumer-facing practices (family, conveyancing, labour, wills and estates, RAF and personal injury) get clients from Google and are where a website pays.

**One legal point.** South Africa's privacy law (POPIA, section 69) covers marketing email to businesses as well as individuals, and attorneys know it. The summaries I read say you may approach a non-customer once to ask for consent. I am not a lawyer and did not read the Regulator's guidance note itself, so treat this as a prompt to check. In practice: keep outreach low-volume and personal, make the opt-out obvious (yours already is), and do not drip a sequence of emails at someone who has not replied. Follow up by phone or in person.

**A rewrite, using Tanners as the example**

> **Subject:** Tanners & Associates: a homepage concept
>
> Hi Mr Tanner,
>
> I build websites for Johannesburg law firms. The most recent is Kochukov & Blume (kblegal.co.za).
>
> I put together a homepage concept for Tanners & Associates, built around your transfer cost calculator, which is the most useful thing on your current site. A screenshot is attached, and the full page is at tanners.virtara.co.za. It is private, uses only what is already public on your site, and is yours to look at with no obligation.
>
> If it is of interest, I can walk you through it in ten minutes, on a call or at your offices in Sandown.
>
> Dylan Petzer
> Virtara, 16 Hume Road, Johannesburg
> 072 327 1040
> Not for you? Reply "no" and I will not contact you again.

What changed: proof in the first line, a screenshot they can see without clicking, a link on your own domain, a compliment that is specific and true, no criticism, no price, a small ask, and a phone number and street address that show you are local and real.

## 3. The strategy

**Lead with the finished thing, to people who already have a reason to trust you, and follow up like a person.**

Most agencies ask a stranger to imagine a website. You can show them theirs. That only works when the right person actually looks, so the plan is built around getting the preview in front of decision-makers: through people who know you first, then through a phone call, and only then through email.

### Why this fits South Africa

- **Trust is the scarce thing.** Business owners here are wary of online scams and of cheap freelancers who disappear. A local number, a street address, named local clients and an offer to meet in person do more than any copy. So does a finished page they can look at before paying.
- **Cash flow decides.** Many small firms cannot find R12,500 this month but can find R1,500. Your competitors know it: WebX sells from R195 a month and Web Partner from R499. Your setup-plus-monthly offer is the right shape; it just needs to be one clear price shown on your site.
- **Business runs on WhatsApp and the phone.** Four of your five competitors have click-to-chat. An owner who ignores email will often answer a call or a WhatsApp from a local number within the hour.
- **Referrals carry weight** in a market this relationship-driven, and you have six clients who have never been asked.
- **December is a deadline you can use.** "Live before you close on the 15th, ready for January" is a true and specific reason to decide now. Your 3–6 day turnaround makes it believable.

### The offer to standardise before you start

Pick one and put it on the site, so the email, the preview and virtara.co.za agree:

- **Website on subscription:** R3,500 setup, then R1,500 a month, hosting and care included, on a 12-month term. Round numbers, the same for everyone.
- **Or once-off:** the existing R12,500 Launch Site.

Do not quote either in the first message. Mention price when they ask, which is the buying signal.

## 4. The 30 days

**Budget: R0 to about R600.** A custom subdomain for previews is free on your existing domain. Optional costs: coffee for meetings, and one networking breakfast.

### Week 1 (11–17 Nov): warm base and repairs

| Day | Do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Create the Google Business Profile for 16 Hume Road. Ask all six clients for a Google review by WhatsApp, with the link. | Free, and every later message is stronger with reviews behind it. |
| 1 | Point previews at your own domain (`firmname.virtara.co.za`) and send from the virtara.co.za mailbox. | Removes the phishing look. |
| 2 | Phone the eight prospects already emailed, starting with Tanners, Snaid & Morris, Total Electric, Secure Plumbing and Skin Derm. Ask for the partner, owner or practice manager by name. | The previews are built. A call is the missing step, and these are the fastest possible wins. |
| 2–3 | Message each past client personally: a thank-you, the care plan and Concierge offer, and a referral ask ("who else do you know whose site needs this? A month's hosting on me if they sign"). | Your cheapest clients are one introduction away. |
| 4–5 | Choose two niches and build a list of 20 in each, with a named decision-maker and a direct number. | See below. |
| 5 | Decide the standard offer and put it on the site. | Ends the two-price-list problem. |

**The two niches**
1. **Consumer-facing attorneys** in Johannesburg north: family, conveyancing, labour, estates. You have proof (KB Legal), built examples and a reason they need enquiries.
2. **Home services and trades**: electricians, plumbers, leak detection. You already have three previews, the owner answers the phone himself, and a missed enquiry is a lost job he can count.

### Weeks 2 and 3 (18 Nov – 1 Dec): the preview play, done properly

Five new previews a week, ten prospects a week in total across the two niches. For each one:

1. **Day 0:** phone first. "I've built a homepage concept for your firm; who should I send it to?" That gets you a name, a direct address and permission, which also answers the POPIA concern.
2. **Same day:** the email above, with a screenshot attached, to that person.
3. **Day 2:** a WhatsApp or call: "Did the concept come through?" Send a 60-second screen recording walking through it.
4. **Day 6:** one last call. Then stop.

Alongside this:
- **Three LinkedIn posts a week:** a before-and-after of a real client site, with one sentence on what changed. Tag the client.
- **Three referral partners:** an accountant or bookkeeper, a signage or print shop, and a photographer who serve small businesses. Offer 10% of the first invoice for an introduction. They meet new businesses every week and get asked "who does websites?".
- **One room of real people:** a Sandton or Randburg business breakfast or a BNI visitor morning. Bring the previews on a tablet.

### Week 4 (2–11 Dec): close

- Every open conversation gets a written one-page quote within the hour of asking, and a deposit link.
- Use the deadline honestly: "If the deposit is in by Friday the 5th, you are live before you close."
- Offer Concierge free for 14 days to anyone hesitating on a full site. It is a small yes that makes them a client.
- Ask each new client for a review and an introduction on the day their site goes live.

## 5. The numbers that tell you if it is working

| Each week | Target |
|---|---|
| Phone conversations with a decision-maker | 10 |
| Previews sent to a named person | 10 |
| Meetings or walkthrough calls | 3 |
| Quotes sent | 2 |
| Google reviews (total by day 30) | 5 |

Expected yield, as a judgement and not a forecast: 40 prospects reached properly should give six to eight real conversations and two to three clients. The warm base and partners should add one or two. If week 2 produces fewer than three conversations, the list is wrong, so change the niche before changing the message.

## 6. What I would not spend on

Google Ads, Facebook ads, bought email lists, and a bigger cold-email tool. With no reviews and analytics only just switched on, paid traffic would be money spent learning what a phone call tells you for free.
