## Post 1

**Title:** How much does a website cost in South Africa?
**Suggested slug:** website-cost-south-africa
**Meta description:** What a business website costs in rands, what drives the price up or down, and what to ask before you sign. Our own prices are listed.

The honest answer is: it depends on what the website has to do. A site that shows people who you are and how to contact you is a different job from a site that takes payments and manages stock. So instead of a vague range, here is how we price it, what changes the number, and what you should ask any developer before you pay a deposit.

### Our prices

We build in three sizes. Each is a once-off build price, not a subscription.

**Launch Site, R12,500.** For startups and service businesses that need a proper web presence. Up to five pages, designed for phones first, with a content management system so you can edit your own text, lead capture forms, core technical SEO and analytics set up. It includes two rounds of revisions and a handover session so you know how to use it. Delivery: 3 to 6 working days once we have your content.

**Growth Site, R28,000.** For businesses with several services that need the site to guide people towards an enquiry. Eight to fifteen pages, a blog and gated downloads, speed work, event tracking, booking or CRM integrations, and a copy framework with conversion recommendations. Delivery: 3 to 6 working days once we have your content.

**Commerce / Custom Platform, from R55,000.** For online shops, member portals and custom workflows. Shopify, WooCommerce or a custom build, with payments, shipping and product setup, automation hooks for email and CRM, staging and testing before launch, and documentation for your team. We quote the timeline up front, based on complexity. The final price depends on scope, which we agree in writing before we start.

On all three, you pay a 50% deposit to start and 50% on completion.

### What changes the price

- **Number of pages.** More pages means more design, more writing and more testing.
- **Who writes the content.** If you supply finished text and photos, the job is faster. If we need to help with the words, it takes longer.
- **Features.** A contact form is simple. Bookings, payments, logins and integrations with other software each add work.
- **Selling online.** Products, stock, shipping rules and payment providers make a shop a larger project than a brochure site.
- **Speed of decisions.** Projects stall when feedback takes weeks. Quick, consolidated feedback keeps the timeline and the cost where they were quoted.

### Costs after launch

A website is not finished the day it goes live. Software needs updating, backups need to run, and someone should notice if the site goes down. We offer optional care plans from R1,000 a month (updates, backups, security scans and uptime monitoring), R2,500 a month (adds performance work, content updates and priority support) and R5,000 a month (adds custom development hours, e-commerce support and a dedicated account manager). You don't have to take one, but ask any developer what happens after launch and who pays for it.

If you want to be found on Google, that is a separate, ongoing job. Our SEO retainers are now SEO + AEO packages, so answer engine optimisation (being named in AI answers) is included, and they run month to month with no lock-in: R4,500, R8,500 or R14,500 a month, depending on how competitive your market is, plus a once-off setup fee. If you also want paid ads, our management fee starts at R6,500 a month, and the ad spend is billed directly by the ad platform, not by us.

### Questions to ask before you pay anyone

1. **What exactly is included?** Pages, revisions, forms, SEO setup and training should be in writing.
2. **How long will it take, and what do you need from me?** A fixed delivery window with your responsibilities listed is a good sign.
3. **How is payment split?** Paying everything up front, or everything at the end, are both worth a pause. A deposit to start and the balance on completion is a fair middle.
4. **Who owns the website, the domain and the content?** The answer should be you.
5. **What happens after launch?** Ask about hosting, updates, backups and support, and what each costs.
6. **Can I see real work?** Ask to see live websites they've built, and who to speak to about them.

### Where to start

If you're not sure which size fits, tell us what the website needs to do and who it is for, and we'll tell you honestly which option makes sense and what it will cost. You can [start a project brief](/start-your-project) or [see all our services and prices](/services).

## Post 2

**Title:** Web design in Johannesburg: what to look for
**Suggested slug:** web-design-johannesburg
**Meta description:** How to choose a web designer in Johannesburg: what to check, what to ask, and the red flags to avoid, from a local studio.

Johannesburg has plenty of people who will build you a website. The harder part is choosing one who will build the right website, on time, for a price you understood from the start. Here is a practical way to decide, written by a studio that works from 16 Hume Road, Johannesburg, so take our own view with that in mind and check everything below against whoever you talk to.

### 1. Start with what the website must do

Before you speak to anyone, write down in one or two sentences what the site is for. "Get more enquiries for my accounting practice from Johannesburg and Sandton." "Sell my products online across South Africa." A good designer will ask you this first. If someone starts with colours and templates before asking about your customers, be careful.

### 2. Look at their own work, properly

Open their portfolio and click through to the live websites. Check them on your phone. Do they load quickly? Is the text easy to read? Is it obvious what to do next? Then ask whether you can speak to one of those clients. A designer who is proud of their results will usually arrange it.

### 3. Ask how the site will be found

A beautiful website that nobody finds is an expensive brochure. Ask what's included for search: page titles and descriptions, a fast-loading site, a Google Business Profile, and the structure that lets Google understand what each page is about. For a Johannesburg business, this should include naming the areas you serve, not just the city. Be wary of anyone who promises a ranking position. Nobody controls Google.

### 4. Get the price, the timeline and the payment terms in writing

You should know, before you commit:

- What the total price is, and what it covers.
- How many weeks the build takes. For reference, our Launch Site and Growth Site are delivered in 3 to 6 working days once we have your content, and for online shops and custom platforms we quote the timeline up front, based on complexity.
- How payment is split. We ask for a 50% deposit to start and 50% on completion, which keeps risk fair on both sides.

For reference, our builds start at R12,500 for a five-page Launch Site and R28,000 for a Growth Site. These are what we charge, and a quote from anyone else should be compared like for like: same pages, same features, same revisions.

### 5. Check who owns what

You should own your domain name, your content and your website files. Ask directly. Some arrangements leave the site on the designer's account, which makes it hard to leave later.

### 6. Ask about life after launch

Websites need ongoing care: software updates, backups, security checks and someone to call when something breaks. Ask what support is included, for how long, and what it costs afterwards. Our care plans are R1,000, R2,500 and R5,000 a month, and they're optional. Whatever you choose, know the numbers before you start.

### 7. Being local helps, within limits

Working with a Johannesburg studio means you can meet in person, they understand local customers and the areas you serve, and you're in the same time zone when something needs attention. It isn't essential, because good work is good work wherever it is made. But for a business serving local customers, local knowledge shows up in the details: the suburbs you mention, the way people search, and the way they prefer to be contacted, including by phone and WhatsApp.

### Red flags

- A price that arrives with no list of what's included.
- No written timeline.
- No live examples, or examples you can't verify.
- Pressure to pay everything immediately.
- Promises of guaranteed first-page rankings.
- Unclear answers about who owns the site.

### The short version

Choose someone who asks about your customers, shows real work, quotes in writing, explains what happens after launch, and lets you own what you paid for.
